## 2. Hardening implemented

1. **Unsigned supersession fails closed** (initial PR, verified): plan-time
   re-attestation rejects any record carrying `supersedes_decision_record_id`
   with the classified `OWNER_PROVENANCE_UNSIGNED_SUPERSESSION` reason. No
   signed supersession protocol was invented; fail closed until a versioned
   protocol is separately designed.
2. **`source_revision` binding** (initial PR, verified): the record's
   `provenance.source_revision` (both the presented provenance and the
   embedded record provenance) must equal the signed input's own; the
   legitimate record-time flow always copies it, so drift is tampering.
3. **Canonical Base64 identity** (initial PR, verified): `_decode_base64`
   re-encodes and compares byte-for-byte, and `owner_provenance_key_id`
   validates before deriving an identity — one key material has exactly one
   representable identity. Compatibility confirmed: every legitimate encoder
   (Python `b64encode`, OpenSSL) emits the canonical form, so no valid key or
   signature is rejected; all 43 focused tests and the full suite pass.
4. **Calendar-invalid dates classify** (initial PR, verified): impossible
   `decided_at` leaves `key_validity=UNKNOWN` → deterministic REJECTED
   dossier; impossible registry validity dates remain classified
   `ValidationError` at model construction (separately pinned by test, as the
   review goal required).
5. **Revocations cannot shape a plan** (R1 review fix, added tests-first in
   `6d5130b`, fixed in `3568c1b`): `plan_profile_activation` blocks with
   `OWNER_REVOCATION_AUTHORITY_UNVERIFIED` whenever a supplied revocation
   withdraws a record of this plan's exact target; a stale/other-target
   revocation stays inert bookkeeping and does not block. Withdrawing a
   decision can never increase what the remaining set authorizes until a
   versioned signed revocation protocol exists.
