# 04 — Evidence-Based Business Quality

> Status: draft specification

## 1. Purpose

Historical financial strength is only useful if the economics can plausibly persist. This module estimates durability without allowing the LLM to rely on vague narrative.

The model uses:

- business hard gates;
- eight fixed quality dimensions;
- quantitative evidence;
- qualitative evidence;
- mandatory counter-evidence;
- confidence and evidence coverage.

Business quality and valuation must remain separate. A great company can still be too expensive.

---

## 2. Business hard gates

A material trigger prevents ordinary `BUSINESS_PASS` and instead returns `FAIL` or `SPECIAL_REVIEW`.

### BG01 — Structural revenue decline

Default trigger:

```text
5Y core revenue CAGR < -5%
AND industry demand is structurally declining
AND no credible replacement earnings engine exists
```

### BG02 — Non-core profit dependence

If sustainable core-business profit is < 50% of normalized total profit and the rest mainly comes from asset sales, subsidies, fair-value changes or other non-recurring sources:

```text
FAIL_OR_SPECIAL_REVIEW
```

### BG03 — Core business cannot generate cash

A core business that persistently fails to create operating cash for 3+ years requires a special model or fails the standard model.

### BG04 — Structural disruption

If >50% of revenue depends on a product being rapidly displaced and the company has no credible transition path:

```text
STRUCTURAL_DISRUPTION
```

### BG05 — Single-point survival dependency

A single customer, supplier, license, platform, commodity input or external variable capable of eliminating >50% of revenue/profit triggers special review.

---

## 3. Eight quality dimensions

Each receives 0–5 points.

```text
B01 Demand durability
B02 Cyclicality
B03 Pricing power
B04 Competitive moat
B05 Capital efficiency
B06 Customer / channel / supplier dependency
B07 Regulation and external dependency
B08 Predictability and business-model simplicity
```

\[
BusinessScore = \sum B01...B08
\]

Maximum: 40.

Critical dimensions:

```text
B01 Demand durability
B03 Pricing power
B04 Competitive moat
B05 Capital efficiency
```

If any critical dimension <= 1, the company cannot receive an ordinary business pass without special review.

---

## 4. Evidence quality

### E3 — Strong primary evidence

Examples:

- audited annual-report data;
- exchange/company formal disclosures;
- directly reproducible 5–10 year financial metrics.

### E2 — Formal operating disclosure

Examples:

- volume, ASP, customer counts, retention, store count, official market share;
- management discussion when tied to verifiable facts.

### E1 — Reliable external evidence

Examples:

- government statistics;
- industry associations;
- high-quality industry research.

### E0 — Inference only

Example:

> The brand is famous, therefore customer loyalty is probably high.

E0 cannot independently support a high score.

### High-score evidence requirement

Suggested default:

```text
Score 4 -> at least 1 E3 + 1 additional E2/E3 equivalent
Score 5 -> at least 2 independent strong evidence items and no severe counter-evidence
Inference-only analysis -> maximum score 3
```

---

## 5. Mandatory counter-evidence

Every dimension must include:

```text
supporting_evidence
counter_evidence
```

The analyst must actively try to falsify the positive thesis.

An empty counter-evidence list is permitted only when the analyst explicitly records which risk sources were checked and that no material contradiction was found.

---

## 6. B01 — Demand durability

Question:

> Why should customers still need this product/service five to ten years from now?

Quantitative diagnostics:

\[
RevenueCAGR_{5Y}
\]

```text
RevenueDeclineYears_10Y
WorstRevenueDrawdown
RecurringRevenueRatio (where applicable)
retention / renewal / repeat-purchase indicators
```

Demand classification:

```text
ESSENTIAL
REPEAT_PURCHASE
REPLACEMENT
DISCRETIONARY
FASHION
ONE_OFF
```

Growth itself is not the score. Persistence of underlying demand is.

Suggested interpretation:

```text
5: long-lived demand, recurring/repeat nature, low substitution risk, recession resilience
4: durable demand with moderate product/competitive risk
3: meaningful economic/product-cycle sensitivity
1–2: significant fashion/substitution/deferral risk
0: demand is structurally disappearing
```

---

## 7. B02 — Cyclicality

Question:

> How much of profit is generated by the business itself versus external cycle conditions?

Prefer 10-year data.

\[
ProfitCV = \frac{\sigma(NormalizedOperatingProfit)}{|\mu(NormalizedOperatingProfit)|}
\]

Also calculate:

```text
MarginDrawdown
LossFrequency_10Y
CoreCDC volatility
```

Reference bands for Profit CV:

```text
<20%      very stable
20–40%    stable
40–70%    moderate cycle
70–100%   strong cycle
>100%     extreme cycle
```

Cyclicality does not automatically mean failure. Strong cyclicals should move into a `CYCLICAL_MODEL` and use full-cycle normalization.

---

## 8. B03 — Pricing power

Question:

> Can the company raise price or pass through cost inflation without losing material volume or share?

Do not infer pricing power from gross margin alone.

Preferred evidence combines:

```text
ASP changes
volume changes
market-share changes
gross-margin resilience
input-cost shocks
```

Strong evidence pattern:

```text
price increase
+ volume broadly stable
+ market share broadly stable
+ margins broadly preserved
```

Brand, industry leadership or patent count alone are insufficient.

Suggested scoring:

```text
5: repeated historical evidence of price increases with resilient volume/share/margins
4: meaningful but imperfect pricing power
3: can broadly pass through costs
1–2: highly commoditized / intense price competition
0: clear price taker
```

---

## 9. B04 — Competitive moat

Question:

> If a well-funded competitor entered today, why could it not easily replicate the economics?

Recognizable moat mechanisms include:

- scale cost advantage;
- network effects;
- scarce licenses/resources;
- switching costs;
- distribution/channel advantage;
- economically meaningful brand;
- defensible technology/IP;
- proprietary data/ecosystem effects.

A claimed moat should manifest in economic outcomes such as:

```text
persistent market share
persistent excess ROIC
high retention
margin advantage
lower acquisition cost
unit-cost advantage
```

### ROIC persistence

Track normalized ROIC and incremental ROIC. A long run of ROIC >15% can be strong evidence only after adjusting for leverage, accounting artifacts and one-off gains.

### Network effect test

The analyst must explain how additional users increase value for existing/other users. User scale alone is not a network effect.

### Brand test

Brand earns moat credit only when it produces premium pricing, higher retention, lower customer-acquisition cost or another measurable economic advantage.

## 10. B05 — Capital efficiency

Question:

> How much capital must continuously be fed back into the business to maintain or grow cash earnings?

### CapEx intensity

\[
CapExIntensity = \frac{\sum GrossCapEx_{5Y}}{\sum AdjustedCFO_{5Y}}
\]

Reference bands:

| CapEx / CFO | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| <20% | extremely light |
| 20–35% | light |
| 35–50% | medium |
| 50–70% | heavy |
| >70% | very heavy |

### Working-capital intensity

\[
NWCIntensity = \frac{NormalizedOperatingNWC}{Revenue}
\]

\[
IncrementalNWCRequirement = \frac{\Delta NWC}{\Delta Revenue}
\]

Negative working capital can be a strength when driven by customer prepayment/supplier terms rather than abusive overdue payables.

### Incremental ROIC

Approximation:

\[
IncrementalROIC = \frac{NOPAT_t - NOPAT_{t-3}}{InvestedCapital_t - InvestedCapital_{t-3}}
\]

This helps distinguish a historically attractive business from one whose new capital now earns poor returns.

Four useful states:

```text
high ROIC + high reinvestment runway -> exceptional compounder
high ROIC + low reinvestment runway  -> mature cash cow; payout matters
low ROIC + high reinvestment          -> dangerous capital consumer
low ROIC + low reinvestment           -> mediocre mature business
```

## 11. B06 — Customer, channel and supplier dependency

Do not evaluate customer concentration alone.

Track:

```text
largest_customer_ratio
Top5_customer_ratio
channel_concentration
critical_supplier_concentration
replacement_time / substitutability
```

Reference for largest customer:

```text
<10%     low concentration
10–20%   acceptable
20–30%   medium
30–50%   high
>50%     extreme
```

A dominant state-owned or government customer may have low credit risk but high bargaining-power risk; do not treat all concentration identically.

Single-platform sales dependence is channel concentration even when end customers are diversified.

---

## 12. B07 — Regulation and external dependency

Question:

> Is regulation primarily a barrier that protects economics, or a policy lever that can destroy them?

Assess two internal dimensions:

```text
BarrierBenefit 0–5
PolicyDamageRisk 0–5
```

Also inspect geopolitical/external dependencies such as export controls, sanctioned customers, advanced-chip restrictions, cross-border data constraints and critical foreign inputs.

High regulation does not automatically mean a low score; stability and direction matter.

---

## 13. B08 — Predictability and simplicity

Question:

> Without forecasting macro variables or stock prices, can we understand how this company will probably make money five years from now?

Track:

```text
Revenue CV
Gross Margin CV
EBIT Margin CV
Core CDC CV
number of unrelated business segments
M&A frequency
accounting complexity
material associates/JVs
fair-value dependence
derivative complexity
```

The analyst must state the 3–5 most important earnings drivers.

Sanity test:

> The company mainly provides ______ to ______, earns money through ______, and relies on ______ as its main economic advantage.

If this cannot be explained clearly in 1–2 sentences, flag `COMPLEXITY_WARNING`.

---

## 14. Score bands and critical-weakness rule

Default bands:

| Business Score | Grade |
|---:|---|
| 35–40 | S |
| 32–34 | A |
| 28–31 | B / PASS |
| 24–27 | WATCH |
| <24 | FAIL |

A high total score does not override a critical dimension <=1.

---

## 15. Evidence confidence

Every final assessment must output:

```text
HIGH
MEDIUM
LOW
```

Evidence coverage:

\[
EvidenceCoverage = \frac{ReliableQuestionsAnswered}{RequiredQuestions}
\]

Suggested thresholds:

```text
>=85%    HIGH
70–85%   MEDIUM
<70%     LOW
```

`LOW` confidence cannot produce an automatic business pass even if the numerical score is high.

---

## 16. Analyst workflow

The LLM must follow this sequence:

```text
1. Explain how the company makes money.
2. Identify major business-model risks.
3. Search for counter-evidence.
4. Gather supporting evidence.
5. Attach quantitative metrics.
6. Score each dimension.
7. Assign confidence and unresolved questions.
```

Do not score first and then search for evidence that confirms the initial view.

---

## 17. Bull / Bear / Adjudicator pattern

For high-priority candidates, use three roles:

### Quality Analyst

Build the strongest evidence-based case for durability.

### Skeptic Analyst

Attempt to falsify it, focusing on demand decay, customer loss, price competition, share loss, margin decay, rising capital intensity, falling incremental returns, regulatory deterioration and acquisition-dependent growth.

### Business Adjudicator

May use only evidence already supplied by the two analysts. It outputs:

```text
FINAL_SCORE
CONFIDENCE
KEY_EVIDENCE
KEY_COUNTER_EVIDENCE
UNRESOLVED_QUESTIONS
```

## 19. Final business gate

```text
IF business hard gate triggered:
    FAIL_OR_SPECIAL_REVIEW

IF BusinessScore < 24:
    FAIL

IF 24 <= BusinessScore < 28:
    WATCH

IF CriticalDimension <= 1:
    FAIL_OR_SPECIAL_REVIEW

IF EvidenceConfidence == LOW:
    MANUAL_REVIEW

IF BusinessScore >= 28
AND EvidenceConfidence != LOW
AND no critical weakness:
    BUSINESS_PASS
```

Business quality never decides `BUY` by itself. It only determines whether historical cash economics are credible enough to proceed to valuation.
